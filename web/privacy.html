Privacy Policy

How Saqua collects, uses, and protects your data. Last updated: 21 July 2026.

This is a clear, plain-English policy written in good faith. It is not yet a lawyer-reviewed document — we intend to have it formally reviewed before general availability. If anything here is unclear, please contact us.

Who we are

Saqua is a B2B sales-research and cold-outreach assistant. It researches companies you point it at, drafts personalized emails, and — when you connect a mailbox and explicitly ask — sends them on your behalf. This policy covers the hosted Saqua product.

What data we collect

  • Account information — your name and email address, provided by our authentication provider (Clerk) when you sign in.
  • Email content you create — the subject lines and bodies of the cold emails and sequences you draft, edit, and (if you choose) send through Saqua.
  • Prospect and research data — the companies and people you ask Saqua to research, and the public information it gathers about them (website content, news, public social posts, and business contact details).
  • Mailbox access tokens — if you connect Gmail or Outlook, we store the OAuth access and refresh tokens needed to send email and detect replies on your behalf. For Gmail we read only message metadata (identifiers and labels), never message content. These tokens are encrypted at rest and are never shown in the interface or sent to your browser.
  • Conversations and workspace state — your chat threads with the assistant and the research/drafts attached to them, so you can return to your work.
  • Operational metadata — usage counts, timing, error logs, and cost/telemetry data we use to run the service reliably and control cost. We do not sell this or use it for advertising.

Google user data (Gmail)

If you connect a Google account, Saqua requests only two Gmail permissions: Send (gmail.send), to send the emails you draft and approve plus their follow-ups; and Metadata (gmail.metadata), to detect replies. The metadata scope grants access only to message and thread identifiers and labels (such as INBOX and SENT) — never the subject or body of any message. Saqua cannot and does not read your email content; when a reply is detected it stores only the message's opaque ID and a timestamp, used solely to stop the follow-up sequence.

Saqua's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Specifically:

  • We do not use Google user data to develop, improve, or train generalized or non-personalized AI or ML models.
  • We do not sell or transfer Google user data, and we do not use it for advertising.
  • We do not allow humans to read your Google user data, except with your explicit consent, where required for security or to comply with the law, or where the data has been aggregated and anonymized.

You can revoke Saqua's access at any time from your Google account permissions or by disconnecting the mailbox on the Connections page, which deletes the stored tokens.

How we use your data

  • To research the companies you ask about and generate grounded, personalized outreach drafts.
  • To send email and detect replies only when you connect a mailbox and explicitly ask us to send.
  • To keep your conversations and drafts available in your workspace.
  • To operate, secure, debug, and control the cost of the service (usage caps, error monitoring, abuse prevention).

We do not sell your data, and we do not use the content of your emails or research to train third-party advertising models.

Third-party services your data passes through

To do its job, Saqua sends the minimum necessary data to specialized providers. Each processes it only to return a result to you:

  • Anthropic (Claude) — page text and prompts, to power research synthesis and email drafting.
  • Firecrawl, Tavily, Exa, Jina — target URLs and search queries, to gather public web and news content about the companies you research.
  • Hunter — company domains, to look up public business contact details where enabled.
  • X (Twitter) API — search terms, to read recent public posts when you ask for recent social signal.
  • Google (Gmail) / Microsoft (Outlook) — your approved email content and the OAuth tokens you granted, to send mail and detect replies.
  • Clerk — authentication and account identity.
  • Our hosting and database providers — to store your workspace and run the application.

We are not responsible for the independent privacy practices of these providers; each publishes its own policy.

Data retention

We keep your conversations, drafts, and research for as long as your account is active so your workspace stays usable. You can delete any conversation at any time, which removes it from our systems. Mailbox tokens are kept until you disconnect the mailbox or delete your account. Operational logs (usage, errors) are retained for a limited period for security and reliability and then aged out. If you delete your account, we remove your workspace data and revoke stored tokens; some minimal records may be retained where required by law.

Security

Mailbox tokens and other secrets are encrypted at rest. API keys live only in our server environment — they are never shown in the interface, never logged, and never sent to the browser. Access to production data is limited to what is needed to operate the service.

Your choices

  • Delete any conversation from your workspace at any time.
  • Disconnect a mailbox to stop all sending and revoke stored tokens.
  • Request access to, or deletion of, your account data by contacting us.
